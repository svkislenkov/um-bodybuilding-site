Hi everyone,

I hope you’re all doing well. I’m excited to share a major update about a new project our club is launching this Winter called the Fitness Access & Empowerment Initiative (FAE). We recently received $5,000 in funding from the School of Kinesiology to support this program, and I wanted to take a moment to explain what it is and how you may be able to get involved.

What Is the Fitness Access & Empowerment Initiative?
FAE is a semester-long program designed to support beginner and underrepresented students who want to get into the gym but face barriers such as intimidation, lack of knowledge, or limited access.

The goal is to help them build confidence, learn proper technique, and develop sustainable fitness habits through:

Theory-driven behavior change

Community support

1-on-1 or small group training with student coaches

Access to structured programming and check-ins

This initiative is a partnership between our club and several faculty members in the School of Kinesiology, including Laura Richardson, PhD, Michele Bird, PhD, Angela Fong, PhD, Andrew Pearson, PhD, and Celina Furman, PhD. It will be a one-of-a-kind program on campus focused specifically on gym accessibility, beginner support, and strength training as a pathway to confidence and well-being.

How You Might Be Able to Get Involved

As part of the program, we want to build a team of student trainers and mentors who can work with participants throughout the semester.

There are two potential pathways to be involved:

1. Get a Subsidized Personal Training Certification (Limited Spots)
We are currently exploring opportunities to subsidize the cost of a CPT (Certified Personal Trainer) certification for club members who want to:

Learn how to coach safely and effectively

Develop real-world experience training and implementing trainer etiquette

Learn from experienced certified trainers

Be part of a meaningful, research-backed campus initiative

If you’ve ever wanted to become a trainer, this could be a great opportunity to do so with clear-cut support.

2. Already Certified? You Can Join the Training Team
If you already hold a CPT from a recognized organization (NASM, ACE, etc.), you may be eligible to join the initial group of trainers working directly with program participants.

This will give you:

Practical coaching experience with advanced training in behavior modification

Programming and other skill development under Kinesiology faculty guidance

Involvement in a passionate campus-wide health initiative

A strong resume and professional development opportunity

When Is This Happening?
The initiative will begin early Winter semester (January - February 2026).
We are still finalizing logistics with faculty, but we want to start identifying interested club members now.

Considering a Club Name Update
As part of this initiative and our broader goal of making strength training more accessible and beginner-friendly, we are also exploring the idea of updating the club’s name to better reflect inclusivity, education, and approachability – while still honoring our roots in strength training.

This is not a final decision, and we want this to be a member-driven conversation. If you have thoughts, concerns, or ideas for alternative names, we’d love to hear from you. We’ll be opening this up for discussion and feedback soon.
